QR-Based Conveyor Sorting Arm

A 3D-printed 6-DOF arm, fed by a conveyor, that identifies parts by QR code and sorts them: a small model of automated line sorting.

Overview

Sorting mixed parts off a conveyor is one of the most common jobs for industrial robots. We built a small version: parts ride a conveyor, a camera reads each part's QR code, and a 6-DOF arm picks it and places it in the right bin.

Demo

How it works

Part on conveyor→QR read→Identify + queue (FIFO)→Pick→Place in bin

What I did

Designed the conveyor

Modeled the conveyor in SolidWorks to feed parts to the arm's pick zone.

Built the arm

Assembled the 3D-printed 6-DOF robotic arm.

Programmed the servos

Wrote the servo control on an Arduino Uno driving a PCA9685 PWM driver.

Added identification and queuing

Implemented QR-based part identification and first-in, first-out handling logic, linking perception to actuation and task scheduling.

Integrated and tested

Brought the arm and conveyor together and ran pick-and-place testing as one cell.

What I learned

Integration is where the work is. Each subsystem worked on the bench, and most of the effort went into timing the conveyor, the QR read and the arm motion so they cooperate.
