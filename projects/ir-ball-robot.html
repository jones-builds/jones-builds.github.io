Overview

The robot follows a line, recognizes IR beacons blinking at 750 or 1500 Hz, approaches the target and fires a ball, all within a 12 × 12 in. footprint and a 2 kg limit.

Mission flow

Line following→Beacon detection→Target approach→Ball launch

Architecture

Split into two subsystems

Two Arduino Uno R4 boards, one for the drive and one for the launcher, coordinating over UART so sensing and launching never block each other.

Designed the IR detection circuit

A PT204-6B phototransistor feeds an LM393 comparator, which turns the beacon's light into a clean digital pulse train the microcontroller can time.

Integrated the sensing

Combined ultrasonic ranging for distance and wheel encoders for motion feedback with the IR detector.

Wrote the controller

A 6-state finite state machine moves the robot from line following through 750/1500 Hz beacon detection to target approach and ball launch.

Built the launcher

A 12 V flywheel launcher spinning at 2000 RPM, reaching 98% single-ball release accuracy.

Laid out the power

Separate 12 V and 5 V rails for the motors and logic.

How the robot tells beacons apart

Beacon frequency discrimination

The comparator output is a square wave. Measuring the time between edges gives the period, and the period says which beacon is in view.

What I learned

Real signals are noisy. Getting reliable beacon detection meant debugging the analog front end and the interrupt timing together, because a fault in either one looks the same from the code.
