Multi-Modal CNC Scrap Analysis

Can a production CNC warn you before it makes scrap? Fusing machine, current and acoustic data to find out, and being honest about the answer.

Overview

Scrap on a production CNC is expensive and is usually found after the fact. With Kirby Risk, I tested whether combining the machine's own MTConnect data with physical sensors (current and sound) could predict scrap from a cycle's signature.

Pipeline

MTConnect→Current (CT)→Acoustic→Time alignment→Cycle segmentation→Defect labeling→XGBoost→Findings

What I did

Collected three data streams

Machine status and spindle load from MTConnect, spindle current from a current transformer, and an acoustic signal from the machine.

Aligned everything in time

Built a Python (pandas, NumPy) pipeline that places all signals on one timeline across 1.3M+ MTConnect records, so each event can be analyzed across every sensor at once.

Segmented cycles

Split the data into 891 machining cycles and set a 17-minute cycle baseline across 3 part programs.

Traced every defect

Matched 96 defects to their root causes: misload, fixture or tooling.

Modeled scrap

Trained XGBoost classifiers on features from the fused signals.

Findings

The model learned the shift, not the physics. Scrap labels were recorded per shift, not per part, so the classifier learned which shift a cycle came from instead of tool-failure behavior. That is label leakage, and it makes accuracy look better than it is.

Even with leakage removed, there were too few examples of each failure mode for reliable prediction. Because most scrap came from misloads and fixtures rather than tool wear, the stronger recommendation was operational: redesign the fixture and improve tool-change practice.

I presented both limits openly to faculty and the plant's engineers. A model that is honest about what the data can support is more useful to a plant than a high accuracy number that won't hold up.

What I learned

In industrial ML, how the data is labeled matters as much as the model. The most valuable output was a clear diagnosis of why prediction wasn't ready yet, and what to fix first on the floor.
